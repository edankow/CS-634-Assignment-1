AI Use and References

How AI contributed

Even Dankowicz developed the product concept, conducted the website research and informal conversations, interpreted reviewed articles, and drafted and edited substantial portions of the content. He also directed the scope, organization, and wording of the presentation. OpenAI Codex assisted with source discovery, drafting and editing, interpretation, evidence organization, HTML/CSS development, and local verification (OpenAI, 2026). The saved HTML files preserve the resulting content, including writing contributed by Even and writing developed with AI assistance.

The prompt records document how Codex was used to develop and revise the writing and website. Research claims are cited to their original sources; the Codex citation acknowledges its writing, analytical, and development assistance.

The separate configuration concept mockup, including its layout, schematic visuals, and browser interactions, was generated with OpenAI Codex assistance (OpenAI, 2026). Its artwork, text lines, prices, and default settings are illustrative.

Accessible supporting records

  • Prompt log: user prompts and output descriptions
  • Content origins and contributions

The prompt log is a historical record, not a list of approved evidence. It includes rejected and superseded ideas. The rejected Reddit material is not used as evidence in this study. Earlier source-review notices are superseded only by specific later approvals.

Course-supplied APA guidance is linked rather than reproduced: Timothy McAdoo’s archived “How to cite ChatGPT”. The original article was supplied by the author; no verified retrieval date for that archived page is asserted. Course prompt-disclosure requirements remain controlling.
